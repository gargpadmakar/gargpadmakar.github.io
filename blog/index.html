Who is Padmakar Garg?

Padmakar Garg is a Senior Android Developer & Mobile Security Engineer with 8+ years of production experience in Kotlin, Java, Jetpack Compose, Kotlin Multiplatform (KMP), and Mobile Application Security. Based in India, he currently serves as a Senior Android & Mobile Security Engineer at Sveltetech Technology Pvt Ltd, engineering high-concurrency real-time messaging, WebRTC audio/video calling (LiveKit), and end-to-end encrypted (E2EE) communications. He specializes in Android Mobile Security, Endpoint Detection & Response (EDR), loopback-VPN traffic inspection, cryptography, and runtime application self-protection (RASP).

What are Padmakar Garg's core technical specializations?

His primary expertise spans: (1) Modern Android Development with Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, Coroutines, Flow, and Kotlin Multiplatform (KMP); (2) Mobile Security & Cryptography (ECDH, AES-GCM, MTProto 2.0, Android Keystore, TLS pinning); (3) Defensive Engineering & RASP (Frida/Xposed hook detection, root & emulator detection, loopback-VPN traffic inspection, DEX tamper verification, and R8 obfuscation); (4) Real-Time Systems (Socket.IO, WebRTC LiveKit, VoIP calling); and (5) Release Engineering & MDM (Jenkins CI/CD, Split APKs, Google Play publishing, and Microsoft Intune MAM/MDM).

What open-source Android libraries and security SDKs has Padmakar published?

Padmakar has authored multiple production-grade open-source SDKs on GitHub: MTProtoCrypto (a pure Kotlin crypto SDK implementing MTProto 2.0 primitives including AES-IGE and Diffie-Hellman), screen-analytics-android (an offline-first screen analytics and navigation tracking SDK), Enterprise Android SecureSDK (a RASP security engine with dynamic tamper defense and policy dashboard), and AppProtectGuard (an Android hardening framework with root and anti-hooking detection).

How does Padmakar Garg implement End-to-End Encryption (E2EE) in Android apps?

He implements E2EE using Elliptic-Curve Diffie-Hellman (ECDH) on secp256r1/X25519 curves for secure peer key exchange, combined with authenticated AES-GCM (256-bit) encryption using fresh cryptographic nonces for every payload. Private keys and session secrets are generated and stored exclusively within the hardware-backed Android Keystore (StrongBox Keymaster) to prevent key extraction even on compromised devices.

What is Padmakar Garg's experience with Kotlin Multiplatform (KMP)?

Padmakar architectures production cross-platform systems sharing business logic, networking (Ktor), offline persistence (SQLDelight/Room KMP), and domain models between Android and iOS, while isolating platform-specific security boundaries (Android Keystore vs. iOS Keychain, Play Integrity vs. App Attest) behind clean expect/actual interfaces.

How does Padmakar architect real-time chat and WebRTC calling on Android?

He architectures resilient, offline-first real-time systems using Socket.IO wrapped in reactive Kotlin Flows with Room DB acting as the single source of truth and optimistic UI updates. For voice and video calling, he integrates WebRTC and LiveKit SDKs with adaptive bitrate control, network reconnection handlers, and background VoIP call notifications via Android Telecom framework and PushKit/FCM.
